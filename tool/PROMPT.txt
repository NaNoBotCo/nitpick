You read one batch of a 360 photo set for Mot Dang (motdang.net), a directory of places in northern Thailand.
The Mac has already levelled every sphere, read every sign with Apple Vision at full camera resolution,
matched signs to Mot Dang records, and cut crops of everything it could not settle. Your job is the part
a machine got wrong or could not see: every legible word, every place, every detail.

Folder: {OUT}
Batch:  {OUT}/batches/{BATCH}.txt   (first line = the image pages; then one block per moment)

Reading the batch file:
  == mNNN <frames> lead <frame> <time> <lat,lng> sweep wNNN.jpg
  near: records within 60 m (id name)
  sNNNN ... = <record>  :: text     settled: OCR matched that record by name; no image. Trust it.
  cNNNN ... tNNN.jpg :: text(conf)  a sign crop on sheets/tNNN.jpg. The OCR text is a guess; read the crop.
  lNNNN ... <label> <conf> lNNN.jpg a subject Apple Vision flagged, on sheets/lNNN.jpg.
  "~<record>" after a sheet = OCR half-matched that record; confirm or reject.
Every line carries its frame and direction (y = yaw, p = pitch, degrees on the levelled sphere).

Every turn re-sends everything already in your context, so turns are the cost. Five turns, no more:
batch file · all pages · all zooms (one Bash) · all zoom images · one Write.

Steps, in this order:
1. Read the batch file.
2. Open every page on its first line in ONE turn (parallel Read calls): sheets/*.jpg and sweep/*.jpg.
   Crops are labelled with their id in a black bar.
3. Read every crop. Then read every sweep band left to right for what no crop covers. Sweep bands are
   low resolution on purpose; they show where to look, not what a sign says.
4. Zoom only when a crop or sweep leaves a fact unsettled:
     python3 ~/.claude/bin/nitpick/nitpick.py zoom "{OUT}" <frame> <yaw> <pitch> --fov 20
   Add --n 1 to see the same direction in the frames either side, and pick the sharper read.
   Use the smallest fov that holds the thing. Decide every zoom from the pages first, run them all in one
   Bash call, open all the results in one turn. Do not open tiles/, band/ or the source photographs.
5. Write {OUT}/reads/{BATCH}.jsonl in ONE Write, one JSON object per line, then reply.

One line per thing found:
{"id": "<crop id, or zoom file name, or sweep:<frame>:<yaw>>", "frame": "...", "yaw": 0, "pitch": 0, "fov": 20,
 "kind": "place|street-art|spirit-house|parking|merch|hanging|hot-spring|rider|fact",
 "name": "<exactly as written, Thai or Latin, tone marks included; empty if not legible>",
 "name_other": "<the sign's other-language name, if any>",
 "what_en": "<noodle shop, tyre shop, wat, mural, PEA box wrap ...>", "what_th": "<same, natural Thai>",
 "record_id": "<a near/~ id when the sign or frontage shows it is that place, else null>",
 "new": true|false, "phone": "", "hours": "", "line_id": "", "web": "", "prices": "",
 "text": "<every other legible word on it, verbatim>",
 "photo": 0-3, "confidence": "high|medium|low"}

Leave out empty fields. "photo" rates the frame as a picture of that thing (0 none, 3 lead picture). yaw/pitch/fov frame it;
the picture is re-cut from the sphere later, so a hand or the rider at the edge costs nothing.
A crop with nothing on it (a reflection, a pattern) or one repeating a place already written gets no line.

Look for, in every sweep, even when no crop points there: spirit houses, murals, graffiti, painted or
wrapped transformer and traffic-light boxes (PEA wrappers too), motorbikes parked in a row, merchandise,
things hanging from ceilings or awnings, hot springs, for-rent and for-sale signs with phones, banners
with an event date, bus or songthaew stops, ATMs, road works, flooding. The rider in a frame looking cool
(visor down) gets kind "rider".

Rules:
- Record what you can see. A name that is not legible stays empty. A record match needs the sign or an
  unmistakable frontage.
- Copy Thai text exactly. Do not transliterate or translate a name.
- The same place in two frames gets one line, from the sharper frame.
- Write only inside {OUT}/reads/. Touch nothing else. Nothing is published from this pass.

Reply in at most four lines: crops read, zooms taken, places (new / matched), and the three best finds.
